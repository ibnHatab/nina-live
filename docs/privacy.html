Privacy policy

Nina (nina-live) · effective 25 November 2026

Nina is open-source software that runs on a device owned by the person or organisation that installs it (its operator): a home or an office. This policy describes what the software does with your data, including data from your Google account when the operator connects it.

Where your data is

  • On the device. Nina's memory (an event log of what was heard, said and done), her settings, and Home Assistant with its sign-ins (including the Google sign-in tokens) are stored on the device itself.
  • No server of ours. The developers of Nina run no service that receives your data. We do not collect, see, sell or share it.

Google user data

When the operator signs in to Google through Home Assistant on the device, Nina can access:

  • Google Calendar: the events of the signed-in account, to tell you your agenda and to create or change the events you ask for;
  • Google Tasks: the task lists of the signed-in account, to read them and to add or complete the tasks you ask for;
  • where the operator enables them, YouTube and Google Photos data that you ask her to find or show.

This data is used only to do what you ask and to remind you of what you scheduled. It is not used for advertising, not sold, not transferred to anyone, not used to train AI models, and not read by people other than those who use the device.

Nina's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Speech and the AI models

To understand what you say and to answer, the device sends your speech, and the text and context needed for a reply, to Google's Gemini API under the operator's own API key; web searches go to a search service on the device or to Gemini with Google Search. Those services handle the data under Google's terms. Nina listens for her name on the device itself, and sends sound only while she is awake.

Your control

  • Turn her microphone or voice off at any time on her screen.
  • Remove her access to your Google account at myaccount.google.com/permissions, or by removing the integration in Home Assistant.
  • Ask the operator to delete her memory: it is a file on the device.

Contact

For questions about the software: open an issue on GitHub. For questions about a particular installation, contact its operator.
